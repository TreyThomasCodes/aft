## intent

ATTENTION-MODEL CLAIMS ARE RATIONALE, NOT ACCEPTANCE. The assertions that agents skip passive numeric trailers, act on imperative event-shaped text, and find aggregates actionable at wake-from-dark are recorded assumptions (owner, measurement window, and null-result response are release gates — see open_questions 11). No test in this campaign falsifies them. The load-bearing justification that stands without them is the honesty argument: never render a number known stale, never render absence as zero, never render zero where no analyzer ran.

2. **Event-shaped alerts replace the retired numeric bar.** The agent-facing numeric bar is DELETED. In its place, the **agent alert block**: one server-rendered `<system-reminder>`-tagged block appended to tool-response text, byte-identical on NDJSON, subc, and MCP (RULED: no per-host formatter, no UI-metadata lane). It renders ONLY when an error-severity diagnostic is a session-new candidate under the predicate fixed in constraints, carrying file:line and the first message — never health counts. (The `(+N more)` coalescing suffix is a count of unshown ALERT IDENTITIES, not a health aggregate, and is not covered by the never-counts rule.) Attribution to the agent's own edit is VERSION-ANCHORED, not time-windowed (RULED), with an epoch fallback where document versions are unavailable, and degrades to neutral wording when another actor last mutated the file. Trigger set is ERROR-DIAGNOSTIC DELTAS ONLY: Tier-2 counts and todos never page this in-context channel; mid-task they are reachable only by an explicit `aft_inspect` call, and at wake by the health digest (move 3). Silence is the default and the honest output.

3. **Since-anchored health digest (second consumer of the same delta engine, management surface).** The delta machinery built for move 2 — canonical error-diagnostic identity, since/snapshot anchoring, and the provably-fresh-or-omit rule — gains a SECOND consumer: `health.digest`, a management-surface operation (args `{root, since}`) discovered by op name and consumed by the dark-wake composer as an ordinary client. It is NOT an agent-facing tool, is never appended to tool-response text, and is never consumed in-context. It carries only signals meeting the same verification tickets as inspect and alerts, and omits the unmeasured; the passive no-server-start bound applies, so a cold root's digest omits diagnostics rather than starting an analyzer. Its trigger set is DELIBERATELY WIDER than the alert channel's (new AND resolved error diagnostics, stat-verified Tier-2 deltas, new TODOs, watcher-observed change summary) — the shared law is verification-or-omit, not a shared trigger set. In scope here: the AFT-side op, its reuse of the delta engine, and its fixtures. Out of scope: the dark-wake event, the wake composer, prefrontal's composition, PLEX's GitHub lane, and all holder-side rendering.

FOUR surfaces are named and must not be conflated downstream: (1) the RETIRED NUMERIC BAR (agent-facing E/W/D/U/C/T tokens and their render paths), (2) the AGENT ALERT BLOCK (this campaign's in-context channel, AFT-owned and byte-identical on all harnesses), (3) the HUMAN FLEET LINE (the other campaign's settled contract, including `aft_status_segment` until open_questions 3), (4) the WAKE HEALTH DIGEST (`health.digest`, management-surface, composer-consumed, explicit pull, never ambient). Remaining human count UIs that read the same snapshot (Pi overlay, OpenCode sidebar lights) are none of these four and are parked in open_questions 4.

## constraints

- **The delta engine is ONE component with TWO consumers, and what is shared is bounded.** SHARED AND BUILDABLE NOW: canonical error-diagnostic identity, the named normalization function, the verification-or-omit filter, and the consumer-scoped snapshot discipline below. NOT SHARED AND NOT YET DEFINED: any durable `since` history older than the session. Consumer A's alert state is explicitly session-scoped and non-persistent, so it cannot back a `since` anchor older than the session; the durable baseline, its retention, its restart behaviour, and the reply when `since` predates retained history are open_questions 7(b). "Delta-engine reuse" therefore names identity + normalization + verification-or-omit + isolation, and may not be read as an implemented since-anchored diff. Consumer A is the AGENT ALERT BLOCK (in-context, session-scoped, errors-only, new-only). Consumer B is `health.digest` (management-surface, `since`-anchored, wider trigger set). SNAPSHOT OWNERSHIP IS CONSUMER-SCOPED AND MUST NOT BLEED, asserted in both directions on ALL THREE alert fields and on every producer partition: a `health.digest` call never reads or writes Consumer A's `live`, `rendered`, or `baseline_established` — so a digest read can neither suppress a later in-context alert, nor flood one by advancing `live`, nor silently establish a baseline — and rendering an alert block never mutates any digest anchor. A shared mutable snapshot is a defect, not an optimization.

- **Alert delivery is host-uniform and server-rendered (RULED).** AFT already owns final response text on NDJSON, subc, and MCP (`response_finalize` / `subc_format`); the alert block is appended there as a `<system-reminder>`-tagged block, riding the same convention as bash completion reminders. No UI-metadata lane, no per-host formatter, no plugin-side renderer — Pi, OpenCode, and MCP text-only receive identical bytes. HARD CAP IS EXACT, NOT APPROXIMATE: at most 3 rendered lines excluding the reminder tags — at most 2 alert lines, plus, when more identities are pending, a third line consisting solely of `(+N more)` where N is the count of UNSHOWN identities. ORDERING IS TOTAL AND DETERMINISTIC: newest-first by the authoritative-observation ordinal in which the identity entered `live` this session (ordinals are session-global across producer partitions, so a coalesced block from two servers still has one total order); identities entering in the SAME observation are ordered by the canonical identity tuple (canonical root-relative file, range start, range end, severity, source/server, code-presence then code, normalized first message). Host-uniform byte-goldens depend on this tie-break. Each alert line is truncated to a fixed maximum length (a release-blocking constant, open_questions 6) with an ellipsis rather than wrapping; a single over-long diagnostic still yields exactly one block. Never multiple reminder blocks on one response.
  DELIVERY IS RECOMPUTED, NOT QUEUED. There is no persistent candidate queue: at finalization of an agent-visible response the deliverable set is recomputed as the union, across the session-and-root's producer partitions, of `live − rendered`. Consequence, stated so it cannot be discovered later: an identity published and then REMOVED by a later authoritative observation before any eligible response is finalized is NOT rendered, is NOT added to `rendered`, and — having been silently pruned — becomes deliverable again if it returns. A queued-event implementation that renders an already-resolved diagnostic violates this rule. Once `baseline_established` holds for a partition, publication of a new identity in it makes that identity deliverable and the next AGENT-VISIBLE response after that publication carries it, so analyzer lag never selects a later ride; publication that IS the first authoritative observation of its partition does not make it deliverable under the default branch below. Internal/transport commands neither carry nor consume a pending alert; the exclusion set is snapshotted verbatim from today's attach exclusion list into a CLOSED fixture list rather than referenced as "whatever the code excludes", so adding an internal command reddens until it is explicitly placed. `aft_inspect` never FORCES a block, but it carries a deliverable block like any other agent-visible response. All identities represented by a finalized block, INCLUDING those counted only by `(+N more)`, are added to their owning partition's `rendered`.

- **Alert predicate (per session × root, PARTITIONED BY DIAGNOSTICS PRODUCER, explicit state machine).** Candidates are ERROR-severity diagnostics only; warnings, info, hints, Tier-2 D/U/C, and todos never page this channel. Diagnostic identity is the tuple (canonical root-relative file, range, severity, source/server, code when present, normalized first message). NORMALIZATION IS DEFINED as: take the first line of the message, trim, collapse internal whitespace runs to a single space, apply NFC — and nothing else. No path rewriting, no numeric elision, no quote folding. The normalizer is a single named function asserted by unit test against a fixed table. Rendered location is the file plus the start line of `range`.
  An AUTHORITATIVE OBSERVATION is server-side acceptance of a version/epoch-verified diagnostics snapshot (rust-analyzer: after quiescence) for a root that already has a live server, or that this inspect just started and verified. Unverified warming reports and passive count reads are NOT observations; the passive path never starts a server to obtain one. A `health.digest` call is NEVER an observation for this machine.
  OBSERVATION SOURCES ARE A CLOSED SET, so the firing condition is testable and cannot silently collapse to inspect-only: (i) the existing post-edit diagnostics wait on this session's mutating tools, for a root whose applicable server was ALREADY live — the wait never starts one, so a cold root stays silent — whose outcome type already carries only version/epoch-verified entries; (ii) diagnostics accepted by a blocking `aft_inspect` under the freshness rules above; (iii) a version/epoch-verified publish accepted into the continuously-drained store for a root whose server is ALREADY live and, for rust-analyzer, quiescent. Nothing else — no timer, no heartbeat, no passive count read, no warming report. Source (i) is what makes an agent edit that breaks the build page WITHOUT an intervening `aft_inspect`; a build in which only (ii) can fire is a defect against this list.
  STATE IS PARTITIONED. Per `(session, canonical root, producer key)` — where the producer key is the same stable server identity used by diagnostics acceptance — the engine holds `baseline_established`, `live` (identities in that producer's last authoritative observation), and `rendered` (identities acknowledged this session for that producer). A ROOT-WIDE `live` SET IS A DEFECT: source (iii) delivers one producer's snapshot, and source (i)/(ii) may complete for some producers while another is pending or exited, so a root-wide substitution would falsely mark unobserved producers' identities `gone`, prune them from `rendered`, and re-page unchanged diagnostics on the next publish.
  An observation updates ONLY the partitions for which a complete version/epoch-verified snapshot was accepted. A post-edit wait with a pending or exited server does not clear, prune, or otherwise mutate that server's partition. A multi-server inspect applies ONE ATOMIC BATCH of partition updates after every server in its snapshotted applicable set has met the freshness rule.
  FIRST authoritative observation OF A PARTITION — DEFAULT branch, and the ONLY branch if open_questions 10 drops the mutation store: set that partition's `live` and `rendered` to its current error identities, set `baseline_established`, render nothing. This is the silent baseline and the ONE case where `rendered` advances without a block; it is what prevents a pre-existing-diagnostic flood. Because it is per-partition, a server first observed later does NOT flood its pre-existing diagnostics merely because another server baselined earlier. ACCEPTED RESIDUAL, recorded rather than discovered later: a session whose first edit precedes any observation of the relevant partition has its own new error absorbed by that baseline and is not alerted; that error is reachable only via `aft_inspect`. Attribution and "edit introduces error" fixtures MUST therefore perform a prior authoritative observation of the same partition before the causative edit and must assert in the fixture that it ran and set `baseline_established`.
  FIRST authoritative observation — GATE-10 KEEP-STORE branch only: within the partition being baselined, identities whose file's last known mutation is owned by THIS session's mutating tools AND whose diagnostic version/epoch >= that mutation are withheld from `rendered` and are deliverable immediately; every other current identity in that partition is baselined. This is the only branch on which a first observation may render a block, and it exists only if the mutation-ownership store is built.
  LATER observations of a partition: let `gone = previous live − current`; set `live := current`; set `rendered := rendered − gone` (silent prune). Disappearance or recovery alone renders nothing. An identity that left `live` and later returns is absent from `rendered` and is deliverable again. No heartbeat re-emit, no force render on `aft_inspect`, no cross-session or cross-root bleed, no persistence beyond the session.

- **Attribution is version-anchored, not time-anchored (RULED).** The attribution clause attaches iff the file's last known mutation is owned by THIS session's mutating tools AND the diagnostic's document version >= that mutation's version; where a server exposes no document version, the epoch is used instead. Analyzer lag is therefore irrelevant. If neither anchor can be established, or if any other actor (worktree merge, bash-mediated git, another session, watcher-observed external edit) owns the last mutation, the clause drops and the alert renders neutrally (`new error in parser.rs:141 (E0308 ...)`). Accepted residual: same-session edit followed by an unobserved external edit to the same file at an indistinguishable version keeps the clause wrongly — content identical, only the clause differs. No wall-clock window exists anywhere in this design.
  - SPIKE (open_questions 10): session-owned mutation tracking on the edit path is NOT evidenced — the evidence shows LSP document versions and per-file post-edit diagnostics only. Do not implement the clause against document versions alone. If the store is absent, building it is in-campaign work, or the owner drops the clause and the attribution acceptance arms are rewritten before implementation. Dropping the store also LOCKS first-observation to the DEFAULT silent-baseline branch above; it does not leave first-observation behaviour unruled.

- **`health.digest` (AFT-side slice), with a verification ticket per category.** A management-surface operation, args `{root, since}`, discovered by op name and consumed by the wake composer as an ordinary client — symmetric to prefrontal's `status.line`. The op NAME is this spec's stable downstream identifier: fixtures, discovery-by-name, and every cross-section reference key on `health.digest`; renaming it is a recorded amendment under open_questions 7(a), not a drafting liberty. It is NOT an agent tool: no agent-facing tool definition, no in-context rendering, no appearance in any tool-response text on any transport, and it neither carries nor consumes a pending alert block. It never starts a server, never blocks on quiescence, and never triggers a cold build.

The same verification-or-omit law binds every `health.digest` reply.
  CONTENT IS TICKETED, not aspirational — "provably fresh or omitted" means each category names the mechanism that proves it: (1) NEW error-severity diagnostics with file:line — ticket: a version/epoch-verified snapshot from an ALREADY-LIVE server (cold root ⇒ category omitted); (2) Tier-2 deltas — ticket: stat-verified artifact identity against the current tree; (3) new TODOs — ticket: the SAME stat-verified Tier-2 artifact that carries them, so a TODO count may appear only when (2)'s ticket holds; (4) watcher-observed change summary — ticket: filesystem events already ACCEPTED into the watcher's journal with the stat identity that accepted them, never an inferred or timer-driven summary; (5) RESOLVED error diagnostics — ticket: a durable baseline that does not exist yet. Category (5) is therefore NOT REPRESENTABLE until open_questions 7(b) rules the durable store: it is OMITTED, not reported as an empty list, and no fixture may assert it representable. Intent's wider-trigger claim is a statement of the ruled DESTINATION for the digest, and (5) is its gated part; nothing downstream may depend on resolution data before 7(b).
  Unmeasured categories are OMITTED (field absent), never zero, never `~`, never `provisional`. The reply is STRUCTURED FIELDS only: no pre-rendered prose, no retired-bar grammar, no `~` marker, no fabricated zero. VERIFIED NUMERIC VALUES ARE PERMITTED where the number IS the datum (stat-verified Tier-2 deltas, TODO counts) — the ban is on rendered count TOKENS, not on structured integers, otherwise the digest could not carry its own required trigger set. The holder composes rendering. SCOPE, stated inline so nothing depends on a cross-reference: in scope are the op, reuse of identity / normalization / verification-or-omit / consumer-scoped snapshots, and fixtures for those properties; out of scope are the dark-wake event, the wake composer, prefrontal's composition, PLEX's GitHub content, all holder-side rendering, and any durable `since` history older than the session. `since` semantics, reply schema, delta baseline/retention, and exposure surface are open_questions 7 and 8; until they land, no digest field name and no durable-baseline behaviour may be depended on downstream.

- **Passive silence on cold roots is a first-class outcome.** Because neither the passive path nor `health.digest` starts servers, a root with no running analyzer produces NO alert and NO digest diagnostics even when an edit breaks the build. This bounds coverage to roots where some in-scope path has already started a server (or, for inspect only, where this call just did). It is correct under omit-not-zero and must never degrade into a rendered `E0` or any clean-state claim.

- **Absence-as-zero is killed at the type, not the formatter — and the AGENT consumer is DELETED, so the obligation is reachability PLUS type shape.** `StatusBarCounts`' non-Option `usize` fields are the mechanism by which absence becomes zero today. Any surviving consumer reads OPTIONAL values; a category with no proven value is absent from the structure, not zero in it. This binds the named server-side sites inside `AppContext::status_bar_counts`: the `todos: tier2.todos.unwrap_or(0)` coercion, and the all-or-nothing `(Some, Some, Some) => ... _ => None` aggregate gate (which today suppresses proven categories whenever any one is unproven — the inverse pathology, equally dishonest). Neither may survive as a silent zero or a silent whole-aggregate suppression. AFTER RETIREMENT THERE IS NO AGENT-FACING CONSUMER OF THIS TYPE AT ALL — the `status_bar` envelope field, both plugin renderers, and both plugin caches are deleted, the alert block carries no counts, and `aft_inspect`'s payload is built by `build_inspect_payload`, not from this type. Downstream must therefore read this as TWO obligations: (1) REACHABILITY — no caller of `status_bar_counts` may reach agent-visible response text or the response envelope on any transport; and (2) TYPE SHAPE — the per-category optional structure holds for the consumers that DO survive (the fleet segment, and whichever human overlay survives open_questions 4), such that a fixture with exactly one proven category carries that category and omits the others. An acceptance arm phrased as "omission on the agent path" is vacuous once (1) holds and must not be written as though a bar still existed. The `status_bar_cached` generation cache must cache the same per-category optional shape, so a cache hit cannot resurrect a zero — or the whole-aggregate `None` — that the uncached path omits. FLEET NEUTRALITY UNTIL OQ 3: `status_bar_counts` also feeds `aft_status_segment`. The type change is therefore performed so that published fleet TEXT is byte-unchanged — any coercion the segment still needs is applied explicitly at the segment's own call site and marked **[GATE-3]**; the `~` marker and any other legacy token inside pre-existing fleet bytes are preserved under that neutrality rule, so the retired-bar goldens must be scoped to the agent bar and the alert channel and may not ban those tokens fleet-wide before open_questions 3 rules. Plugin parsers (`parseStatusBarCounts`, `readNumber`) must not coerce missing fields to zero on any path reaching a human overlay (overlay fate is open_questions 4).

- **Named retirement surface** (downstream must be able to locate each, server side AND plugin side). DELETED — `refresh_status_bar_counts` on the inspect path; the server-side emit-on-change gate `AppContext::should_emit_status_bar` together with its `status_bar_last_emitted` state (agent-bar cadence machinery, no other consumer); `STATUS_BAR_HEARTBEAT_CALLS = 15` and plugin `shouldEmitStatusBar` (unchanged-state re-emission is the pathology); `formatStatusBar` / `statusBarLine` as agent renderers; `statusBarSuffixForSession` (OpenCode) and `statusBarBlockForSession` (Pi) including their `force = true` render on `aft_inspect`; `parseStatusBarCounts` zero-coercion on the agent path; plugin `captureStatusBar` / `getStatusBar` / `lastStatusBar` on BOTH the NDJSON bridge and the subc transport (caches of the retired envelope field); the solo-bar byte-goldens; `_aft_suppress_status_bar` AS AN AGENT-BAR MECHANISM only. REMOVED FROM THE ENVELOPE — the `status_bar` response-envelope field and its insertion in `attach_status_bar` (the alert block is server-rendered into response TEXT, so the envelope carries no agent-facing counts).
  MUST SURVIVE — `attach_status_bar` may NOT be deleted wholesale: it is also the sole site that publishes `aft_status_segment` to the fleet plane and computes `holder_owns_status_bar`, both fenced fleet lane; the fleet publish is MOVED, NOT DELETED, and must remain reachable under an explicitly named symbol after the agent-bar body is removed. `AppContext::status_bar_counts`, `update_status_bar_tier2`, and the `StatusBarCounts` type likewise may not be deleted while open_questions 3 and 4 are unruled — the fleet segment and both human overlays read them; they are CONVERTED to per-category optional values under the constraint above, fleet-text-neutral, and any decision to delete them requires those rulings first. OPEN — `aft_status_segment` text honesty (open_questions 3); the Pi `StatusBar` / `readStatusBar` overlay AND the OpenCode `shared/status.ts::readStatusBar` + `tui/sidebar.tsx::collapsedHealthLights` overlay, which today renders green "ok" on zero-read counts (open_questions 4); the fleet-publish consequence of retiring `_aft_suppress_status_bar`, whose early return currently also suppresses the fleet publish for cross-root responses (open_questions 9).

- **Fleet lane.** The human fleet line — prefrontal's stamper, the publisher, the catalog gate, and the harness-scoped suppress (merged, ac5ffbfd, the change that made the fleet line render on exactly one harness) — is the settled contract of the other campaign and is not reopened here beyond open_questions 3 and 9. Until those are ruled, no campaign-wide honesty claim may be asserted, and no retired-bar assertion may reach into fleet-published bytes. Alerts and the digest must COMPOSE with the fleet line, never duplicate it, and the human lane must not reintroduce agent-facing count tokens by fallthrough. COMPOSE HAS A TESTABLE MEANING, pinned by the host-uniformity arm: on a response carrying both, the alert block renders byte-identical to its fleet-absent form, and the response contains no agent-facing count tokens other than the fleet line itself.

- **Observation records are server-side, and RENDER ALONE CANNOT ANSWER THE QUESTION.** Because the alert channel is byte-identical across NDJSON, subc, and MCP, AFT writes the durable records at the moment of the event, covering every harness. TWO record kinds are authored here, because the committed offline question ("was a rendered alert's diagnostic resolved within N turns?") cannot be answered from render events plus a join key alone: (1) an ALERT-RENDERED record written at finalization, carrying alert id, session id, root, producer key, tool-response id, diagnostic identity fingerprint, file:line, severity/code, attributed-vs-neutral form, render turn ordinal, and the lifecycle join key; and (2) a DISAPPEARANCE record written when a previously rendered identity leaves `live` at an authoritative observation of its partition, carrying the same join key, session, root, producer key, observation ordinal, and the agent-visible turn ordinal in effect. Record (2) is measurement-only: it never renders and never pages the alert channel. A design that omits (2) must instead name an EXISTING durable diagnostics-transition source and prove by fixture that it joins on producer and lifecycle key without parsing rendered prose; "a join key" is not such a source. A committed offline query over `opencode.db` is ONE consumer of these records for the OpenCode measurement window; it is not their home, and alerts rendered on Pi or MCP text-only must appear under the same schema. CI asserts the record schemas and that the query runs against fixtures; it never asserts an efficacy result. This constraint fixes AUTHORSHIP and SCHEMA only — the storage sink for non-OpenCode sessions, retention, and query surface remain release gates under open_questions 11.

- **Prefix-cache discipline.** Alerts render only on change by construction. `health.digest` is not an agent tool and does not itself bust the tool-description prefix cache. The alert-channel explanation shares the ONE release boundary and prefix-cache bust with the blocking-fresh inspect semantics.

- **No new config knobs, no new AGENT-FACING tools, no per-model tuning.** Selected timeout, reap, and line-length values are product constants for this release, fixed in code and asserted in tests once chosen. `health.digest` is a management-surface operation and is the SOLE addition to the operation namespace in this campaign; it adds no agent tool, no config key, and no per-model behaviour. Registration, authorization, and transport exposure for it are in scope for the AFT-side slice and are pinned by open_questions 8.

- **All transports.** Identical inspect and alert semantics on standalone NDJSON, subc, and MCP (text-only), verified independently per transport for timeout propagation, polling, interruption, shutdown, and exactly-once terminal delivery. The surfaces on which `health.digest` is reachable are open_questions 8; wherever it is reachable, it is reachable identically.

The alert channel consumes fresh-Tier-2 verdicts as provided by the blocking-fresh inspect campaign's stat-verification machinery.

## acceptance_sketch

**Retired numeric bar**
- Byte-goldens prove that ordinary responses and `aft_inspect` responses contain no instance of the RETIRED BAR GRAMMAR (the former bracketed AFT health bar in its exact structural form), no `~` staleness marker in any AFT health rendering, no `status_bar` response-envelope field, no 15-call heartbeat, and no plugin-injected duplicate. THE ASSERTION IS GRAMMAR-SHAPED, NOT LETTER-SHAPED: a companion fixture proves that a diagnostic code such as `E0308` inside an alert line, and ordinary prose containing the letters E/W/D/U/C/T, remain legal — a token ban on those letters would contradict the alert contract and must itself redden. The goldens are SCOPED to the agent bar and the alert channel and may not reach into fleet-published bytes while GATE-3 is open.
- Mutation arms restore each retired renderer or emit site independently, and each must redden at least one targeted golden: `formatStatusBar`, `statusBarLine`, `statusBarSuffixForSession`, `statusBarBlockForSession`, `shouldEmitStatusBar`, the `force = true` render on `aft_inspect`, server `refresh_status_bar_counts`, server `should_emit_status_bar` (with `status_bar_last_emitted`), and plugin `captureStatusBar` / `getStatusBar` / `lastStatusBar` on NDJSON and subc.
- REACHABILITY arm (obligation 1, and the reason the omission arm below is not vacuous): a static/instrumented arm asserts that NO caller of `AppContext::status_bar_counts` reaches agent-visible response text or the response envelope on any transport, and that `aft_inspect`'s payload is produced by `build_inspect_payload` rather than from that type. Re-wiring any surviving caller into `response_finalize`, `subc_format`, or the envelope must redden this arm.
- TYPE-SHAPE arm (obligation 2, SERVER side, SURVIVING consumers): unit arms pin that the `todos: tier2.todos.unwrap_or(0)` coercion and the all-or-nothing `(Some, Some, Some) => ... _ => None` aggregate gate no longer produce a silent zero OR a silent whole-aggregate suppression for the consumers that survive — a fixture with exactly one proven category must carry that category and omit the others, which the old gate could not do. A companion arm drives the same fixture twice so the `status_bar_cached` generation cache is exercised, asserting a cache hit reproduces the same omissions rather than resurrecting a zero or the whole-aggregate `None`. With no applicable or installed analyzer, neither the passive channel nor any agent-facing payload may fabricate `E0 W0`; Tier-2-unproven states omit D/U/C. Absence is asserted by optional-field absence, and a plugin fixture proves `parseStatusBarCounts` / `readNumber` do not coerce missing fields to zero on any path reaching a human overlay.
- **[GATE-3]** Fleet-text neutrality arm, written now and branch-neutral: after the type change, the bytes published as `aft_status_segment` are IDENTICAL to the pre-change bytes for the same fixture, including whether a segment publishes at all when only one category is proven, and including any `~` or other legacy token already present in those bytes. Any coercion the segment still needs is asserted to live at the segment's own call site. Changing fleet-visible text or publish conditions before open_questions 3 rules reddens this arm; the arm is rewritten to the ruled behaviour once it lands.
- Fleet-publish survival: an arm asserts `aft_status_segment` is still published to the fleet plane and `holder_owns_status_bar` is still computed after the agent-bar body is removed, reachable under an explicitly named surviving symbol. Deleting `attach_status_bar` wholesale, or deleting the `status_bar_counts` accessor it reads, must redden this arm. **[GATE-3]/[GATE-9]** the segment's own text honesty and the cross-root fleet-publish consequence of retiring `_aft_suppress_status_bar` are unruled; no arm asserts either outcome, and no campaign-wide honesty claim is asserted in CI until they land.
- **[GATE-4]** Pi `StatusBar`/`readStatusBar` and OpenCode `shared/status.ts::readStatusBar` + `tui/sidebar.tsx::collapsedHealthLights` are unruled. One arm is written now and is branch-neutral: neither overlay may render a clean/green state from an absent or unproven value. Survive, delete, or re-bind is decided later; absence-as-clean is forbidden under every branch.

**Alert channel**
- Silent baseline: a session opened against a fixture that already has errors, whose first authoritative observation precedes any this-session edit, renders no alert on that observation and none on a subsequent unrelated call. A companion arm proves the baseline observation SET `rendered` (not merely suppressed the render), by showing an identity present at baseline still does not fire on a later observation.
- Observation-source arm (pins the closed source list and specifically that the channel does not collapse to inspect-only): after a baseline observation, a session mutating-tool edit that introduces an error fires exactly one block with NO intervening `aft_inspect` call, driven by the post-edit verified diagnostics wait alone. A second variant drives source (iii) — a verified publish accepted for a root whose server is already live and quiescent — and also fires. A build in which only an explicit inspect can produce an observation reddens this arm. A negative variant proves a warming (pre-quiescence) report and a passive count read are NOT observations, and that the post-edit wait started no server on a cold root.
- PARTITIONING arms (a root-wide `live` set is a defect, asserted directly): on a two-server root, (i) an observation that accepts a verified snapshot from server A only updates A's partition and leaves B's `live` and `rendered` untouched — a build that substitutes a root-wide `live` will falsely mark B's identities `gone`, prune them from `rendered`, and re-page them on B's next publish, which this arm must catch; (ii) a post-edit wait with server B pending or exited does not clear, prune, or otherwise mutate B's partition; (iii) a server first observed later does NOT flood its pre-existing diagnostics merely because another server baselined earlier — its own first observation is its own silent baseline; (iv) a multi-server inspect applies ONE ATOMIC BATCH of partition updates only after every server in its snapshotted applicable set met the freshness rule, so no partial batch is observable from a concurrent finalization.
- RECOMPUTED-NOT-QUEUED arm: an identity published and then REMOVED by a later authoritative observation before any eligible agent-visible response is finalized is NOT rendered, is NOT added to `rendered`, and — having been silently pruned — fires normally if it returns later. A queued-candidate implementation that renders an already-resolved diagnostic reddens this arm. A companion arm asserts the deliverable set is the union of `live − rendered` across the session-and-root's partitions at finalization time, so a coalesced block may span two producers.
- First-observation branch arm, exactly one of two, never both green: DEFAULT branch — a fixture that edits a diagnostics-bearing file and then takes its FIRST authoritative observation of that partition renders NO alert for the newly introduced identity, which is in `rendered`, and the error is reachable only via `aft_inspect` (the accepted residual, asserted rather than discovered); **[GATE-10]** KEEP-STORE branch — the same fixture renders exactly one block with the your-edit clause, and every non-attributable identity present in that partition is still baselined. The fixture names which branch it encodes; a branch flip without a recorded ruling is a contract violation.
- Silent prune and re-entry: an identity that leaves `live` and later returns fires again exactly once; a mutation that keeps pruned identities in `rendered` must redden it. Disappearance or recovery alone renders nothing.
- Identity normalizer: a unit arm runs the single named normalization function against a fixed table (first line, trim, whitespace-run collapse, NFC — and nothing else). Table entries prove that path rewriting, numeric elision, and quote folding do NOT occur; adding any of them must redden.
- **[GATE-10]** Attribution, three arms, mutation-proofed both directions. Every arm expecting a block after an edit FIRST performs an authoritative observation of the SAME PARTITION that establishes the baseline (asserted in the fixture, including that `baseline_established` was set), THEN applies the causative mutation. (i) a session mutating-tool edit introducing an error renders exactly one `<system-reminder>` block on the FIRST AGENT-VISIBLE response after that diagnostic is published — not necessarily the next one after the edit — with file:line, first message, and the your-edit clause; (ii) an external/bash-mediated or watcher-only mutation of the same file renders the same block with NEUTRAL wording and no clause; (iii) an unrelated call with no session-new error renders no block at all. Arms (i) and (ii) presuppose a session-scoped last-mutating-tool + document-version/epoch store that is NOT evidenced today. If the spike finds no store and the owner does not build one, (i) and (ii) are REWRITTEN to the neutral-only form and the your-edit clause is deleted from copy and goldens; they may not be left green by asserting on document version alone.
- **[GATE-10]** Analyzer-lag arm: after baseline, a diagnostic published three eligible tool calls after the causative edit still alerts and still attributes (version >= mutation version). Forcing a longer lag must not change the rendered clause or select a later ride. Withdrawn with the clause under the drop branch; the alert-still-fires half survives either way.
- Coalescing arm: two or more new identities accumulated between renders produce exactly ONE block, ordered newest-first by the session-global observation ordinal in which each entered `live` (ordinals span producer partitions, so a two-server coalesced block still has one total order), with same-observation ties broken by the canonical identity tuple (asserted by a fixture in which two identities enter in the SAME observation, so a non-deterministic order reddens the byte-golden), with the exact `(+N more)` count of UNSHOWN identities, within the exact 3-line cap (at most 2 alert lines plus the `(+N more)` line, excluding reminder tags). Assert block count == 1 and that the next unchanged response is silent. Every identity represented by the finalized block, INCLUDING those counted only by `(+N more)`, is asserted to be in its owning partition's `rendered` afterwards.
- Truncation arm: a single over-long diagnostic yields exactly one block, one line, ellipsis-truncated at the fixed maximum line length, never wrapped. **[GATE-6]** the constant is unruled; the arm asserts the truncation property now and pins the number in a golden once chosen.
- Re-emission arm: an already-rendered identity does not re-fire; reintroducing the 15-call heartbeat or the force-on-inspect render must redden a golden. A companion arm proves `aft_inspect` CARRIES a pending block like any other agent-visible response while never forcing one.
- Exclusion arm: internal/transport commands neither carry nor consume a pending alert — a pending block survives an excluded call and rides the next agent-visible response. The excluded set is asserted against a CLOSED list snapshotted verbatim into the fixture from today's attach exclusion list, not against whatever the code excludes at run time: an internal command outside the snapshot that neither carries nor consumes reddens the arm, so adding a command forces a recorded placement decision instead of drifting silently.
- Isolation arms, both directions: no cross-session and no cross-root bleed; alert state does not persist beyond the session (a new session against an unchanged tree re-establishes a silent baseline rather than firing). If a durable digest baseline store exists (open_questions 7b), an arm proves the alert channel does not read it.
- Cold-root silence arm: no analyzer running for the root plus an edit that breaks the build ⇒ no block, and specifically no `E0` or clean-state rendering anywhere in the response. The passive path is asserted to have started no server.
- Host-uniformity arm: byte-golden proves identical rendered block text on NDJSON, subc, and MCP text-only, with no host-side reformatting, no UI-metadata lane, and no second injection path, and never more than one reminder block on one response. A companion golden with the fleet line present on the same response proves the block renders byte-identical to its fleet-absent form and that the response carries no agent-facing count tokens other than the fleet line itself — this is the testable meaning of COMPOSE.
- Trigger-set arm: a Tier-2 count delta or todo change with no new error diagnostic renders no block; the same delta is visible via `aft_inspect`. Warnings, info, and hints likewise never page this channel.
- Alert/aggregate split: no project-wide counts in the alert channel; `aft_inspect` remains the aggregate surface with its category set and `sections` contract unchanged.

**Delta engine, two consumers**
- Snapshot isolation, asserted in BOTH directions on the same fixture, across ALL THREE alert fields, and on EVERY producer partition: a `health.digest` call for a (session, root) never reads or writes `live`, `rendered`, or `baseline_established` in any partition — a later agent-visible response still fires the block for that identity, a digest call cannot itself establish a silent baseline, and a digest call cannot advance `live` so as to flood a later observation — and rendering an alert block never mutates any digest anchor. A shared mutable snapshot must redden both arms.

**`health.digest` (AFT-side slice only)**
- Not-an-agent-tool arm: no agent-facing tool definition is registered, the op never appears in tool-response text on any transport, and a digest call neither carries nor consumes a pending alert block. The op is reached by NAME (`health.digest`); the fixture keys on that name so a rename reddens rather than silently re-binding.
- Passive-bound arm: a digest call against a cold root starts no server, never blocks on quiescence, never triggers a cold build, and OMITS diagnostics rather than reporting zero or `~`.
- Verification-ticket arms, one per category: (1) new error diagnostics appear only from a version/epoch-verified snapshot of an ALREADY-LIVE server; (2) Tier-2 deltas appear only under stat-verified artifact identity; (3) a TODO count appears only when (2)'s ticket holds on the same artifact; (4) a watcher change summary appears only from events already accepted into the watcher journal with the stat identity that accepted them — an inferred or timer-driven summary reddens the arm; (5) RESOLVED diagnostics are OMITTED entirely, not reported as an empty list, and no fixture asserts them representable while GATE-7(b) is open.
- Structured-fields arm: the reply carries no pre-rendered prose, no retired bar grammar, and no `~` marker. Verified numeric values (stat-verified Tier-2 deltas, TODO counts) ARE permitted and are asserted present in the Tier-2 fixture — an assertion banning integers outright would contradict the wider-trigger arm and must itself redden.
- Wider-trigger arm: on a root with a live server, stat-verified Tier-2 deltas, new TODOs, and a watcher change summary are all representable — categories the alert channel must not carry.
- **[GATE-7]/[GATE-8]** No wire schema, field name, `since` type/boundary, ordering, retention, restart-survival, or exposure/authorization behaviour is frozen in a golden. Fixtures assert only the properties above. In particular no arm depends on a `since` anchor older than the session until the durable baseline store is ruled; "delta-engine reuse" is asserted only as reuse of identity, normalization, verification-or-omit, and consumer-scoped isolation — never as a wire claim and never as reuse of the session alert snapshot as a `since` baseline.
